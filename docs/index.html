OpenWASH
A reuse-safety guardrail

The line between reuse that heals and reuse that harms is a handful of numbers.

OpenWASH holds those numbers honestly — the corroborated thresholds that separate safe reuse of human waste from a new disease vector — and puts them where the decision is made.

Why it matters

Closing the loop can feed people — or reinfect them.

The suffering is fecal–oral disease. Diarrhea is still a leading killer of children under five, and soil-transmitted helminths — Ascaris, the roundworm this engine is built around — infect on the order of a billion people, stunting growth and cognition.

Reusing excreta as safe soil or fuel is one of the few things that makes sanitation affordable and wanted in poor places. But done wrong, reuse is a new disease vector: unsafe sludge on a food crop reinfects the very people it was meant to help. That failure is documented — deployed ecosan latrines in Malawi whose 12-month sludge still exceeded the safety limit.

The entire margin between helping and harming is a few safety numbers — and those numbers are scattered across dense PDFs, easy to get wrong, and exactly what an AI will confidently hallucinate.

The guardrail

An honest answer — including “I don’t know.”

Describe what you have, how it was treated, and where it’s going. OpenWASH returns one of three verdicts, with the binding pathogen, the specific fix, and the limits it never hides. It says unsafe when it should, and not enough evidence when the science is thin — the opposite of a confident guess.

UNSAFE
faeces · 6-month passive storage · 25 °C · crops eaten raw

Passive storage at 25 °C needs ≥ 365 days to reach ≤1 viable egg/g; you have 180. Add urine/urea/ash (pH ≥9) to sanitize far faster, or heat-compost ≥50 °C. Deployed passive storage has under-performed in the field, so treat this bound as a floor.

SAFE — by screen
faecal sludge · urine/urea, ~200 mM measured dose, pH 9.1 · 30 °C · held 60 days

The measured ammonia dose clears Ascaris in ~29 days; you held 60. The dose must be measured — pH alone (e.g. lime) can’t confirm it. Still a screen, not a certificate — confirm by measuring ≤1 egg/g before reuse, and it says nothing about chemical hazards.

NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE
compost · held at 45 °C

45 °C sits in a gap with no robust helminth time–temperature curve in the evidence. Reach and hold ≥50 °C, or treat it as ambient storage instead. The tool refuses to invent a number it can’t source.

The moat

Numbers you can trust because you can check them.

Every value is enforced against four rules. This is what makes the substrate different from a spreadsheet of thresholds — and what caught real over-claims in its own sources.

  • 01 / provenanceNo naked numbers. Every value cites a source and page. A number without provenance never gets committed.
  • 02 / corroborationTrust is computed, not asserted. The engine counts independent source lineages — it caught a WHO target that looked triple-sourced but was one derivation echoed three times.
  • 03 / realityDesign target ≠ field-verified. The guideline value and whether deployment actually achieves it are separate fields. One passes in the field; one is contradicted.
  • 04 / honestyContested values are surfaced, never averaged. When two respected sources disagree, both are kept and resolved by policy — the average is nobody’s recommendation.

The honest conclusion

Safe reuse of human waste is a subsidized public good — not a business that pays for itself.

A full techno-economic study took one reuse pathway down to the pipe and the household fee. The engineering is cheap and solvable; treatment is a rounding error. No product pathway self-funds it — recovered nutrients have almost no market, and fuel covers a fraction. The honest question was never how to make it profitable. It’s who funds it — and increasingly, the sector agrees.

Where it fits

The verification layer nobody else is building.

WHO, EAWAG, and the ISO standards define the numbers — but ship them as prose PDFs and paywalled, lab-based certification. Nobody makes provenance, field-verification, and “not enough evidence” a first-class, open, point-of-decision tool. Meanwhile the sector is moving to outcome-based finance — governments and funders paying on verified safe outcomes — with no cheap, trustworthy way to verify them.
