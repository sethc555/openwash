# OpenWASH — an open, provenance-tagged reuse-safety substrate and guardrail for excreta reuse

**Author:** Seth Cope — Independent researcher (ORCID: [0009-0000-5520-915X](https://orcid.org/0009-0000-5520-915X))

## Abstract

OpenWASH is an open, provenance-tagged constraint substrate and decision guardrail for the safety
of reusing human excreta, urine, and wastewater. It consolidates the corroborated thresholds and
pathogen die-off kinetics that separate safe nutrient-cycling reuse from a disease vector, and
enforces four honesty rules on every value: no naked numbers (each cites a source and page); a
corroboration tier computed from independent source lineages (which caught a real over-claim in the
guidelines); a separation of design-target from field-verified outcome; and contested values
surfaced rather than averaged. A first-order die-off engine screens storage, ammonia-amendment, and
thermal treatment regimes for pathogen-reduction sufficiency (binding on Ascaris), siding with the
conservative signal, and a plain-language guardrail returns SAFE / UNSAFE / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE with
the binding pathogen and the specific fix. Load-bearing numbers were adversarially verified against a
151-paper refutation scan. A companion techno-economic study finds the engineering cheap and solvable
and that safe excreta reuse is a subsidized public good rather than a self-funding business. Data are
sourced (WHO, EAWAG, Sphere, US EPA, peer-reviewed literature) and cited, not rehosted.

## Status

OpenWASH is a screen, not a certifier — a decision aid that rules out unsafe reuse designs and states what to measure. It is not a medical device, a guarantee of safety, or a substitute for testing before reuse, and it does not address non-pathogen (chemical / antimicrobial-resistance) hazards.
